## Group observations

**Cross-skill patterns.** These four are a single coherent unit — the v0.3 Commercial Model quartet — held together by two spines: the six-way product-type classification from v0.2 (each skill has a "Product Type × X" matrix: metrics, features, pricing, targeting), and the ID cross-reference chain (KPI→FEA→BR loops). Each skill repeats the same four structural blocks: Consumes/Produces restatement, a Product Type matrix, an anti-pattern table, an output-requirements checklist, and a confidence-boilerplate stanza. Roughly a third of each SKILL.md is small-context-model scaffolding (workflow position breadcrumbs, restated context, duplicated example entries) rather than method. There are also stale seams worth fixing during consolidation: "HORIZON workflow" naming survives in three of four headers, moat-definition and pricing-model disagree on their mutual ordering, and both point to a "Moat Articulation" skill that doesn't exist.

**Strongest distilled-experience material.** (1) The **anti-columns** — Anti-Metrics per product type (outcome) and Anti-Models per product type (pricing) — telling you what NOT to measure/charge is rarer and more valuable than the positive guidance. (2) The **quantified switching-cost inventory** and the **behavioral-inertia/Excel-workaround analysis** in moat-definition — the "your real competitor is a spreadsheet and its moat is habit; attack at pain-threshold moments with first value under 5 minutes" chain is the best single page in the group. (3) The **SMB Penalty calculation** and Pricing Pattern Library ratios. (4) The **numeric tripwires**: >40% P2/P3 scope-bloat kill, >$5K/>40hr switching-cost materiality, CAC payback ≤3 months entry floor, ≥1 Tier-1 metric. (5) The **benchmarks.md** number tables (needs re-dating, but the per-product-type benchmark selection is the durable part).

**Consolidation opportunities.** (a) Merge the two 5-tier evidence scales (moat's Evidence Quality Guide, pricing's WTP Hierarchy) into one kernel evidence-tier convention — they're the same idea with different labels, and both end in the same rule: no decisions on Tier 4-5. (b) Hoist all four Product Type × X matrices into a single product-type strategy reference consumed by every commercial playbook, so v0.2 classification fans out once instead of four times. (c) Pool benchmarks.md + pricing's WTP calibration data + moat's switching-cost thresholds into one dated, versioned commercial-benchmarks reference library. (d) All four "Output Requirements" checklists are ready-made **policy packs** for `check` (mvp-scope-integrity, moat-evidence-floor, kpi-gate-linkage, wtp-before-price-lock). (e) The natural v2 shape is one `shape --playbook=commercial-model` umbrella journey doc sequencing four playbooks (outcome-metrics → moat-and-switching-costs → pricing-model → feature-value-mapping), which also resolves the current ordering contradictions.
