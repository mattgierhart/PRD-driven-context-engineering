## Group observations

**Two generations of skill-authoring are visible.** Runbook-creation is old-generation: multi-file, a ~170-line example duplicated verbatim inside SKILL.md, and reference-file pointers that have rotted (`runbook-examples.md` / `incident-framework.md` cited; `examples.md` / `incident-response.md` exist). Changelog, drift, and MOPS are new-generation: single lean file, explicit upstream credits (AgriciDaniel, alirezarezvani, jonathimer), boundary-fencing against sibling skills, and consistent anti-pattern tables. The v2 packaging should look like the newer three.

**Strongest distilled-experience material in this group**: (1) the runbook reference library — the 9 troubleshooting decision trees with per-path time estimates, the irreversible-migration rollback rule, and the escalation ladder with per-level authority grants; (2) the MOPS SLA benchmarks + rejection-routing discipline; (3) the drift-vs-alerting conceptual boundary and recalibrate-trigger concept. All three carry specific numbers and named failure modes no model produces unprompted.

**Consolidation opportunities**: (a) Drift is already the group's de facto kernel primitive — MOPS delegates SLA decay to it, changelog engagement is listed as a subject, AEO re-testing is its worked example; promoting baseline/compare/history to `learn` kernel behavior removes three cross-references at once. (b) Both GTM-ops skills carry "runs before v0.9, reconcile placeholders later" caveats — direct evidence the linear v0.X ordering fights the real dependency graph and supports the v2 move to verbs + planes with the ordering as an optional journey doc. (c) The anti-pattern tables across all four are the same rhetorical instrument (pattern / signal / fix) and could share one registry format. (d) Severity/escalation definitions in runbook-creation should be defined once (shared with monitoring-setup) rather than restated; the runbook quality gates and MOPS quality gates are both natural `check` policy packs (`ops-readiness`, `lead-lifecycle-readiness`).
